Jupiter · ESA

JANUS on JUICE

ESA's Jupiter Icy Moons Explorer launched in April 2023 and reaches Jupiter in July 2031. Its JANUS camera uses UK-built detectors supplied through the Open University to map Ganymede, Callisto and Europa. I am writing up two signal-dependent effects in its CIS115 CMOS sensor: a baseline shift and negative trailing.

  • launched 2023
  • arrives 2031
  • CIS115 CMOS

Venus · ESA

EnVision

ESA's next mission to Venus. The Open University is helping build VenSpec-U, an ultraviolet spectrometer that will study sulphur chemistry in Venus's atmosphere and hunt for its unknown UV absorber. Our team tests and characterises the detectors at the heart of the instrument.

Mars · international

I-MIM

The International Mars Ice Mapper is a proposed multi-agency orbiter to find accessible water ice ahead of human exploration. The Open University is developing a core detector that combines visible and infrared sensing in one package, so its imager can tell water ice from carbon-dioxide ice.

  • visible + infrared
  • water vs CO₂ ice

What I do

Two ends of the same photon

Every discovery in astronomy starts with light from a distant source landing on a detector. I have worked at both ends of that journey.

The detector

Building the eyes

At the Centre for Electronic Imaging I characterise the CCD and CMOS sensors that fly on planetary missions: photon-transfer curves, read noise, dark current and the subtle signal-dependent effects that decide whether a flight camera returns a clean image.

The sky

Chasing explosions

Before moving into instrumentation I studied gamma-ray bursts, superluminous supernovae and the magnetars that may power them. I found afterglows with the GOTO wide-field telescope, classified transients in real time and modelled what drives the most energetic explosions we see.

Research

Key papers I led

My first-author papers, plus two where I am second author and one of the corresponding authors. Pick a theme, or open the full list of all my publications below.

MNRAS 544, 1541 · 2025First author

Discovery and analysis of afterglows from poorly localized GRBs with the Gravitational-wave Optical Transient Observer (GOTO) All-sky Survey

Most Fermi and MAXI bursts come with error regions too large for normal telescopes to search. GOTO's wide field found the optical afterglows of seven of them in 2024. With redshifts from the VLT and GTC, radio detections and broadband modelling, the paper shows that optical discovery of such bursts favours luminous events with hard spectra.

  • 7 long GRBs
  • z ≈ 0.40–3.16
  • Ejet ~ 1051–1052 erg
MNRAS 538, 659 · 2025First author

Signatures of the shock interaction as an additional power source in the nebular spectra of SN 2023ixf

The first supernova spectrum taken with WEAVE on the William Herschel Telescope. One year after the explosion, broad side-wings on Hα show the ejecta still being energised by collision with material the star shed a few hundred years before it died.

  • +363 d
  • Hα wings at ±5650 km s−1
  • shock power ≳ 5×1040 erg s−1
  • MZAMS ≲ 12 M☉
MNRAS 531, 3297 · 2024First author

Magnetars as powering sources of gamma-ray burst associated supernovae, and unsupervized clustering of cosmic explosions

I modelled the light curves of 13 GRB supernovae and two relativistic broad-lined Ic supernovae with a spinning-down magnetar engine. Unsupervised machine learning then showed that GRB supernovae occupy their own region of magnetar parameter space, separate from superluminous supernovae, fast blue optical transients and long and short GRBs.

  • 15 SNe modelled
  • median Pi ≈ 20.5 ms
  • Mej ≈ 5.2 M☉
  • ~95% clustering accuracy
New Astronomy 116, 102346 · 2025Sole author

Insights from modelling magnetar-driven light curves of stripped-envelope supernovae

Magnetar fits to 11 stripped-envelope supernovae. The superluminous SNe 2010kd and 2020ank need the fastest-spinning, weakest-field magnetars. Explosion energies above about 2×1051 erg point to a possible jittering-jet mechanism. Correlation analysis and PCA map how the events differ.

  • 11 SESNe
  • Eexp ≳ 2×1051 erg
  • correlations + PCA
New Astronomy 97, 101889 · 2022First author

Tale of GRB 171010A/SN 2017htp and GRB 171205A/SN 2017iuk: Magnetar origin?

Late-time imaging with the 3.6 m Devasthal Optical Telescope of two GRB supernovae, one among the brightest and one among the faintest known, together with their prompt gamma-ray emission. A magnetar engine reproduces both, and also the only known GRB-associated superluminous supernova, SLSN 2011kl.

  • z = 0.33 and 0.037
  • 3 GRB–SNe modelled
  • 3.6 m DOT
MNRAS 502, 1678 · 2021First author

SN 2020ank: a bright and fast-evolving H-deficient superluminous supernova

One of the brightest hydrogen-poor superluminous supernovae, with a fast, symmetric light curve. A magnetar spinning once every 2.2 ms fits it, and its spectra closely resemble the fast-evolving SN 2010gx.

  • z = 0.2485
  • Mg ≈ −21.84 mag
  • Lmax ≈ 3.9×1044 erg s−1
  • Pi ≈ 2.2 ms
ApJ 892, 28 · 2020First author

SN 2010kd: Photometric and spectroscopic analysis of a slow-decaying superluminous supernova

A nearby, slowly fading superluminous supernova like PTF12dam, followed from four weeks before peak to six months after. Its light curve favours interaction with circumstellar matter, and its spectra show O II and Si II at two different velocities. It lives in a dwarf galaxy that is forming stars rapidly.

  • z = 0.101
  • −28 to +194 d
  • O I mass ≲ 10 M☉
J. Astrophys. Astron. 43, 27 · 2022First author

Photometric calibrations and characterization of the 4K×4K CCD Imager, the first-light axial port instrument for the 3.6m DOT

Gain and read noise checked for every gain and readout-speed setting, colour terms and zero points tracked over five years, and extinction and night-sky brightness measured in ten filters at Devasthal.

  • 5 gains × 3 readout speeds
  • 10 filters
  • 2016–2021 data

As second author and one of the corresponding authors

MNRAS 507, 1229 · 2021Second author · co-corresponding

Photometric, polarimetric, and spectroscopic studies of the luminous, slow-decaying Type Ib SN 2012au

More than a year of photometry, polarimetry and optical and near-infrared spectroscopy of one of the most luminous slow-fading Type Ib supernovae. A magnetar powers it, the polarimetry shows asymmetric ejecta, and the late spectra point to a Wolf–Rayet progenitor.

  • −0.2 to +413 d
  • Mej ≈ 4.7–8.3 M☉
  • MZAMS ≈ 17–25 M☉
J. Astron. Instrum. 12 · 2023Second author · co-corresponding

4K×4K CCD Imager for the 3.6m DOT: Recent up-gradations and results

Instrument details not covered in earlier papers. A Zemax analysis traced the vignetting and ghosts at the image edges, which a redesigned light baffle now fixes. The paper also covers finite-element design of the filter housing, filter-wheel upgrades, and fringe correction in the I, i and z filters. New science includes Wolf–Rayet stars in M101, an Abell galaxy cluster and GRB host galaxies.

  • Zemax vignetting/ghosts
  • FEA filter housing
  • fringe correction I, i, z
  • 9 WR stars in M101
