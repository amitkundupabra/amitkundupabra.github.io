01About

From transient skies to flight detectors

I am a Spaceflight Imager Engineer at the Centre for Electronic Imaging (CEI), School of Physical Sciences, The Open University. I test and calibrate imaging sensors for ESA and NASA planetary missions, including the CIS115 CMOS sensor of the JANUS camera on JUICE, as well as I-MIM and EnVision. The work runs from optical and vacuum test setups in an ISO Class 5 cleanroom to photon-transfer analysis, noise and dark-current measurements, and Python pipelines that turn test data into detector performance.

My background is observational astrophysics. I did my PhD at the Aryabhatta Research Institute of Observational Sciences (ARIES) in Nainital, India, on the connection between gamma-ray bursts and supernovae, then held postdoctoral positions at the University of Warwick with the Gravitational-wave Optical Transient Observer (GOTO) and at Royal Holloway, University of London. Instrumentation has been part of my work throughout: my first-author papers include the photometric calibration of the 4K×4K CCD Imager on India's 3.6 m Devasthal Optical Telescope.

02Research

Two threads, one instrument chain

JANUS / JUICEI-MIMEnVision

Imaging detectors for planetary missions

Characterising CCD and CMOS sensors so that flight cameras return calibrated, trustworthy images.

  • Photon-transfer curves, conversion gain, read noise, linearity and full-well capacity
  • Dark current and noise measurements in vacuum test setups
  • Signal-dependent effects in the CIS115 sensor for JANUS: baseline shift and negative trailing (two SPIE papers in preparation)
  • Cleanroom handling and contamination control for sensitive detectors
GRBsSupernovaeGW counterpartsGOTO

Time-domain astrophysics

Finding fast-evolving transients early, following them up, and working out what powers them.

  • GRB–supernova connections and magnetar-powered explosions
  • Superluminous and stripped-envelope supernovae; late-time shock interaction
  • Afterglow discovery for poorly localised GRBs with GOTO's wide-field survey
  • Light-curve and spectral modelling (MOSFiT, TARDIS, SYN++) and machine-learning classification
